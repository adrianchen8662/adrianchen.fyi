I'm working toward the Northeast 115: every 4,000-foot peak in the Adirondacks, the Catskills, New Hampshire, Maine and Vermont. This post is where I keep track, with a map of all 115 summits and a table of what I've hiked and what's still ahead.

## The list

The Northeast 115 is the [Northeast 111](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_111) with four peaks added. The list is still called the 111 because the name is older than the additions: later surveys found that Galehead and Bondcliff in New Hampshire, and Redington and Spaulding in Maine, do reach 4,000 feet. It's maintained by the [AMC Four Thousand Footer Club](https://www.amc4000footer.org).

| Group | Peaks |
| --- | --- |
| New Hampshire (the White Mountains 48) | 48 |
| Adirondack High Peaks (the 46) | 46 |
| Maine | 14 |
| Vermont | 5 |
| Catskills (Slide and Hunter) | 2 |
| **Total** | **115** |

A few quirks come with the list:

- The Adirondack 46 is a traditional list. Four of its peaks (Blake, Cliff, Couchsachraga and Nye) are under 4,000 feet by modern surveys but still count, which is why the true number of 4,000-footers is 111.
- Tecumseh in New Hampshire was resurveyed in 2019 at 3,997 feet, and is still on the White Mountains list.
- Not every summit has a maintained trail. Owl's Head, Redington and Spaulding, and a good number of the Adirondack peaks (the Seward Range, Allen, Marshall and others), are herd paths or bushwhacks. The table has a filter for them.

## About the numbers

Summit elevations come from the lists on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_111), and the map markers are placed from published summit coordinates, so a few can sit a short way from the true summit on the map. Length and elevation gain describe one standard route to each summit, round trip. I've rounded them, and I take them as a guide to the size of the day rather than a measurement. Several peaks share a route, such as Lafayette and Lincoln on the Franconia Ridge loop, so their rows repeat the same outing instead of adding to it. Check a current guidebook or the trail's managing agency before you go.

The map tiles are [OpenTopoMap](https://opentopomap.org), drawn from OpenStreetMap data.
